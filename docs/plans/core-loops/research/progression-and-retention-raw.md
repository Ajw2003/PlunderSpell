# Raw research 6: incremental progression and how long players stay

Added 2026-10-25 at the owner's request: "incremental progression towards an actual end goal, not
just survival", with Cookie Clicker and Scritchy Scratchy named as favourites. Raw data only.
Sources are listed at the end of this file.

## How long players stayed: monthly average players on Steam (SteamCharts, read 2026-10-25)

| Game | Launch-window month (average) | Best month (average) | Latest full month (average) | Fall from best month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lethal Company | Nov 2023: 38,974 | Dec 2023: 107,897 | Sep 2026: 2,654 | 97.5% |
| R.E.P.O. | Mar 2025: 105,384 | Mar 2025: 105,384 | Sep 2026: 16,858 | 84.0% |
| PEAK | Jun 2025: 41,147 | Aug 2025: 62,372 | Feb 2026: 16,178 (Steambase; Mar 2026 17,835) | 74.1% |
| How to Fish | Aug 2026: 148,647 | Aug 2026: 148,647 | Sep 2026: 32,419 | 78.2% in one month |

Spikes after the launch window, all within a month of a content update or a sale:

- Lethal Company: Aug 2024 peak 43,580; Apr 2026 average 7,085 (from 3,683 the month before).
- R.E.P.O.: Nov 2025 average 53,071, peak 193,551 (a cosmetics update, per a 4Gamers headline);
  May 2026 average 48,178, peak 115,319.
- PEAK: Nov 2025 and Jan 2026 rebounds (Steambase).
- How to Fish: "the latest patch ... may not give lapsed players a reason to return, since fixing
  problems differs from adding something new" (DLCompare, 11 Sep 2026, when it was at about 27,000).

Why players left How to Fish, from that article's readers: the islands were exhausted and the bosses
beaten; "just a fishing game"; similar games keep players with randomised runs; getting friends
online together is the real barrier. Review sentiment (Signal and Circuit): the loop of fish, kill,
sell, upgrade "is fun at first but lacks long-term engagement"; few species; the same islands and
shop rotations.

## Cookie Clicker's progression, as built (Wikipedia)

- Click for one cookie; cookies buy 20 kinds of building that make cookies on their own.
- Each building costs 15% more than the last of its kind, so costs grow exponentially.
- Upgrades boost clicks and buildings. Golden cookies appear at random, fade in seconds, and grant
  a temporary effect when clicked. Wrinklers eat production but pay out when popped.
- Sugar lumps (from one billion cookies) level buildings and unlock minigames. 637 achievements
  unlock cosmetic milk colours and "kitten" multipliers.
- Ascension resets the run for heavenly chips and prestige levels: +1% production each, plus
  prestige upgrades. The cookies needed for the next level rise with the cube of the level.
- Critics: "flying" (IGN); an "illusion of progress" (Destructoid); players keep a "silly" pursuit
  going for hundreds of hours (Sebastian Deterding).
- Numbers: 50,000 players within hours (Aug 2013); 1.5 million hits in one day; 60,000+ concurrent
  on Steam after the Sep 2021 release.

## Scritchy Scratchy (Lunch Money Games, 18 Mar 2026)

- $6.99 on Steam, also on mobile. Developer: over 750,000 copies in under a month (17 Apr 2026,
  all platforms). Raijin estimate for Steam alone: about 386,000 units, about $2.2 M gross; Steam
  peak 23,780; 94% positive of 17,489 reviews.
- Loop (Kotaku): wash dishes for the first cash, then scratch lottery tickets. Bigger wins buy
  pricier tickets that pay more. Upgrades: a coin that scratches faster, paid "luck". Automation: a
  robot that scratches, and a fan that feeds it tickets. Some tickets hide penalty symbols that cost
  money if fully uncovered, so you scratch just enough; the robot scratches everything, so one bad
  ticket can cost $200,000 or more.
- Playtime on the top reviews: 61.5, 27.5, 23.5, 13.4, 12.4, 11.4, 7.8, 6.4 (reached 100%), 5.2 h.
- Praise: "way more addictive than it should be"; jackpot anticipation; choosing when to risk a
  dangerous card; slow start that picks up once upgrades arrive.
- Criticism (13.4 h, 193 found it helpful): once automation arrives, manual scratching becomes
  pointless and the game turns into tapping robots while numbers climb; the demo covers most of it.

## Persistent progression in roguelikes (Game Maker's Toolkit, "Roguelikes, Persistency and Progression")

- With no carry-over (Spelunky), only skill moves you forward; failed runs can feel wasted.
- With persistent power (Rogue Legacy, Dead Cells), every run helps, but difficulty falls over
  time: "Did you get better, or did the game just get easier?"
- Progress that does not change balance: new options in the drop pool (Enter the Gungeon), new
  characters with trade-offs (Nuclear Throne), cosmetics (Downwell), story revealed by failing
  (Hades), shortcuts (Spelunky), temporary carry-over (Into the Breach).
- Keeping skill relevant: Rogue Legacy's Charon takes all unspent money before the next run; Dead
  Cells loses unspent cash on death unless it is banked at an upgrade station.
