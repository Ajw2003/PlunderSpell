# Roadmap

Full technical doc: [Roadmap.md](../../2-roadmap/Roadmap.md)

## What it is
The eight milestones the project is built toward, what counts as finishing each, and how much
each is worth toward the whole.

## Why it matters
Without a fixed definition of "done", progress becomes opinion. Weighting each milestone lets the
project's progress be worked out rather than guessed.

## How it works
1. Restore normal gravity after forking from an earlier project.
2. Prove speaking spells feels good, with real people of different accents on real microphones.
3. Get the whole loop working for four players in one castle, finishing a raid together.
4. Give every historical age its own castle rooms, loot, enemies and weapons, with none borrowed
   from another age.
5. Build the home base as a real place, and a market where leftover takings are spent and stay
   bought.
6. Make the enemies feel alive: they react when hit, come to look at noises, hunt players who
   run, and can be crept past by crouching, which is slower and quieter.
7. Make the castle itself dangerous, with working doors, stairs, traps and a boss.
8. Last, one pass for animation, sound, final art and speed, ending with someone new installing
   the game and playing it with a friend.

Game systems come first. Expensive art, animation and sound wait for the last milestone, so they
are made once, after what they dress has stopped changing. Cheap touches like screen shake can go
in any time.

## Risks and safeguards
- **A milestone called finished from a unit test alone.** Most need a real person playing.
- **"Done" drifting over time.** When a finish line changes, the roadmap says what it used to
  say and why it moved.
